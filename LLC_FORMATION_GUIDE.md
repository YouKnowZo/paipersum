# Complete LLC Formation Guide for PaiperSum

## Why Form an LLC?

### Benefits
1. **Personal Liability Protection** - Separates personal assets from business liabilities
2. **Credibility** - "PaiperSum LLC" looks more professional than sole proprietorship
3. **Tax Flexibility** - Can choose how you're taxed
4. **Business Bank Account** - Required for Stripe, easier accounting
5. **Legal Shield** - Especially important for legal tech business

### When to Form
- **Now:** If you're launching and accepting payments
- **Wait:** If you're just testing/validating (can operate as sole proprietor initially)
- **Recommendation:** Form LLC before accepting your first paying customer

## Step-by-Step Formation Process

### Step 1: Choose Your State

**Best States for Online Businesses:**

#### Delaware
- **Pros:** Business-friendly courts, privacy, no state income tax if not operating in DE
- **Cons:** Annual franchise tax ($300), need registered agent ($100-300/year)
- **Cost:** ~$500-700/year
- **Best For:** Seeking investment, planning to scale big

#### Wyoming
- **Pros:** Lowest costs, strong privacy, no state income tax, no franchise tax
- **Cons:** Less established case law
- **Cost:** ~$150-250/year
- **Best For:** Bootstrap founders, digital nomads

#### Your Home State
- **Pros:** No foreign qualification needed, easier compliance
- **Cons:** State-dependent costs and taxes
- **Cost:** Varies widely ($50-800)
- **Best For:** Operating locally, want simplicity

**Recommendation for PaiperSum:**
- **If bootstrapping:** Wyoming or your home state
- **If seeking investment:** Delaware
- **If revenue < $50k/year:** Your home state for simplicity

### Step 2: Choose a Business Name

**Name Requirements:**
- Must include "LLC" or "Limited Liability Company"

### Step 3: File Formation Documents

#### DIY Method (Cheapest - $50-200)

**Delaware Example:**
1. Go to: https://corp.delaware.gov/
2. File "Certificate of Formation"
3. Pay filing fee ($90)
4. Get registered agent ($100-300/year)

**Wyoming Example:**
1. Go to: https://wyobiz.wyo.gov/
2. File "Articles of Organization"
3. Pay filing fee ($100)
4. List Wyoming address (registered agent $50-150/year)

**What You Need:**
- LLC Name
- Registered Agent (address in the state)
- Member/Manager info
- Purpose of business: "Legal technology services"

#### Online Service Method (Easier - $100-500)

**Recommended Services:**
1. **Northwest Registered Agent** ($39 + state fees)
   - Professional
   - Includes registered agent first year
   - Great support
   - Website: northwestregisteredagent.com

2. **Inc Authority** ($0 + state fees)
   - Free filing service
   - Makes money on add-ons
   - Website: incauthority.com

3. **LegalZoom** ($79 + state fees + $299/year)
   - Most known
   - More expensive
   - Good support
   - Website: legalzoom.com

**What They Do:**
- Prepare and file all documents
- Act as registered agent
- Ensure compliance
- Handle state correspondence

### Step 4: Get Your EIN (Tax ID)

**What It Is:**
- Employer Identification Number
- Like a Social Security Number for your business
- Required for bank account, Stripe, taxes

**How to Get It (FREE):**
1. Go to: https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online
2. Fill out Form SS-4 online
3. Get EIN instantly (if during business hours)

**What You'll Need:**
- LLC formation documents
- Your SSN
- Business address

**Time:** 10-15 minutes  
**Cost:** FREE (don't pay anyone for this!)

### Step 5: Create Operating Agreement

**What It Is:**
- Internal rules for your LLC
- Not filed with state (private document)
- Proves separation from personal finances

**Single-Member Operating Agreement Template:**

```
OPERATING AGREEMENT OF PAIPERSUM LLC

This Operating Agreement is entered into as of [DATE] by [YOUR NAME], sole member.

ARTICLE 1: ORGANIZATION
1.1 Name: The name of the LLC is PaiperSum LLC
1.2 Purpose: Legal technology services and document analysis
1.3 State: [Delaware/Wyoming/Your State]
1.4 Duration: Perpetual

ARTICLE 2: MANAGEMENT
2.1 Management: Member-managed LLC
2.2 Authority: Member has full authority to conduct business

ARTICLE 3: CAPITAL CONTRIBUTIONS
3.1 Initial Contribution: $[AMOUNT] by [YOUR NAME]

ARTICLE 4: DISTRIBUTIONS
4.1 Distributions: At discretion of Member

ARTICLE 5: TAXES
5.1 Tax Treatment: Disregarded entity (default for single-member LLC)

ARTICLE 6: DISSOLUTION
6.1 The LLC may be dissolved by written consent of the Member

Signed: ___________________
[YOUR NAME], Member
Date: ___________________
```

**Where to Get Templates:**
- Free: Rocket Lawyer (free trial), LegalZoom templates
- Paid ($50-200): State-specific attorneys, LegalZoom full service

### Step 8: Set Up Accounting & Compliance

#### Accounting Software (Choose One)

**Wave (Free - Recommended for Starting)**
- Pros: Completely free, invoicing, receipts
- Cons: Limited features
- Cost: $0/month
- Best for: Revenue < $50k/year

**QuickBooks Online ($30/month)**
- Pros: Industry standard, full features, integrations
- Cons: Monthly cost, learning curve
- Cost: $30/month
- Best for: Revenue > $50k/year

**FreshBooks ($17/month)**
- Pros: User-friendly, great for service businesses
- Cons: Limited inventory management
- Cost: $17/month
- Best for: Simplicity preferred

#### Annual Compliance

**Federal:**
- File annual tax return (Form 1065 if multi-member, Schedule C if single-member)
- Due: April 15 or October 15 with extension

**State:**
- Annual report/franchise tax (varies by state)
- Delaware: $300/year (June 1 deadline)
- Wyoming: $60/year + registered agent
- Most states: $50-300/year

**Reminders:**
- Set calendar alerts for deadlines
- Use Northwest Registered Agent compliance calendar
- Consider tax software or accountant

### Step 9: Get Business Insurance

**Types You Need:**

#### 1. General Liability Insurance ($300-600/year)
- Protects against: Property damage, bodily injury claims
- When: Once you have revenue or customers
- Where: Hiscox, NEXT Insurance, Simply Business

#### 2. Errors & Omissions Insurance ($800-2,000/year)
- Protects against: Professional mistakes, negligence claims
- **CRITICAL for PaiperSum** - users might claim faulty analysis
- When: Before launching or at $50k revenue
- Where: Hiscox, Embroker, CoverWallet

#### 3. Cyber Liability Insurance ($1,000-3,000/year)
- Protects against: Data breaches, cyber attacks
- When: When storing user data
- Where: Embroker, Coalition, At-Bay

**Recommendation for Year 1:**
- Start: General Liability ($500/year)
- Add at $50k revenue: E&O Insurance
- Add at $100k revenue: Cyber Liability

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Mixing Personal & Business Finances**
   - ALWAYS use separate accounts
   - Can "pierce the corporate veil"

2. **Not Getting EIN**
   - Required for bank account, Stripe, taxes
   - Free from IRS directly

3. **Skipping Operating Agreement**
   - Proves business is separate entity
   - Important for liability protection

4. **Ignoring Annual Requirements**
   - Late fees pile up
   - Can lose LLC status

5. **Wrong Tax Classification**
   - Single-member: Usually S-Corp after $50k profit
   - Consult CPA

6. **No Insurance**
   - LLC protects personal assets
   - Insurance protects business assets
   - Need both

7. **Using Personal Address**
   - Consider privacy implications
   - Use registered agent address where possible

## Tax Considerations

### Single-Member LLC (Default)

**Federal:**
- "Disregarded entity" - flows through to personal taxes
- Report on Schedule C of Form 1040
- Pay self-employment tax (15.3%)

**When to Consider S-Corp:**
- Revenue > $50,000/year
- Profit > $40,000/year
- Can save 30-40% on self-employment tax
- Requires: Payroll, quarterly taxes, more paperwork

### Multi-Member LLC

**Federal:**
- Partnership tax return (Form 1065)
- Members receive K-1
- Pay self-employment tax on earnings

**State:**
- Varies by state
- Most: Income flows through
- Some: State LLC tax (CA = $800/year minimum)

**Recommendation:**
- Year 1: Keep as single-member, disregarded entity
- Year 2: If profit > $50k, talk to CPA about S-Corp election
- Use tax software or CPA for filing
